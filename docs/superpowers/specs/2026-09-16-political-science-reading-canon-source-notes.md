# Political Science Reading Canon — Source Notes

Date: 2026-09-16

This note records the bibliographic corrections discovered during design self-review. It is not a second design authority; the companion design spec remains authoritative for architecture.

## Verified corrections

- David Collier, *Working with Concepts: Foundational Essays by David Collier, with Research Notes on Innovation in the Field* (Cambridge University Press, 2026), edited by Zachary Elkins.
- David Waldner, *Qualitative Causal Inference and Explanation* (Cambridge University Press, 2026).
- *The Entrenchment of Democracy: The Comparative Constitutional Design of Elections, Parties, and Voting* is an edited volume edited by Tom Ginsburg, Aziz Z. Huq and Tarunabh Khaitan (Cambridge University Press, 2024).
- *Political Parties and the Crisis of Democracy: Organization, Resilience, and Reform* is edited by Thomas Poguntke and Wilhelm Hofmeister (Oxford University Press, 2024).
- Sarah Brierley, *The Co-opted State: How Politicians' Control Over Bureaucrats' Careers Threatens Governance* (Cambridge University Press, 2026).
- Noah L. Nathan, *The Scarce State: Inequality and Political Power in the Hinterland* (Cambridge University Press, 2023).
- Henry Farrell and Abraham Newman, *Underground Empire: How America Weaponized the World Economy* (Henry Holt and Company, 2023).
- Caroline Kuzemko, *Climate Politics: Can't Live with It, Can't Mitigate without It* (Cambridge University Press, 2026).
- John J. Mearsheimer and Sebastian Rosato, *How States Think: The Rationality of Foreign Policy* (Yale University Press, 2023).
- Jeff Gill and Le Bao, *Bayesian Social Science Statistics: From the Very Beginning* (Cambridge University Press, 2024).
- Clifford Young and Kathryn Ziemer, *Polls, Pollsters, and Public Opinion: A Guide for Decision-Makers* (Cambridge University Press, 2024).
- Myles Williamson, Christopher Akor and Amanda B. Edgell, *Democracy in Trouble: Democratic Resilience and Breakdown from 1900 to 2022* (Cambridge University Press, 2024/2025 publication cycle).

## Design consequence

Bibliographic roles must be modeled explicitly. A reading-canon record should distinguish `authors`, `editors` and, where relevant, `contributors`. Edited volumes must not be flattened into authored monographs. Theory units extracted from edited volumes should retain chapter-level provenance when a claim or argument belongs to a specific chapter author rather than to the editors collectively.
