# Historie / Forskning og akademia — Role World source-first

## Scope

This is a dedicated one-role realism rollout for `historie/historie_forskning_og_akademia` after its career foundation became `playable`. The prerequisite package is authoritative for entry, work loops, People, Places, Mail, Knowledge, consequences, performance, progression, exit and the persistent `forskningslogg_og_manusspor`. This rollout authors only the remaining realism dimension: **situated_reputation**.

It is explicitly **not** a new runtime, a new scene format, a new qualification system, a new academic hiring system, or a cross-role world. The existing Scene Pipeline remains canonical.

## Sociological problem

Historical research is not only an epistemic activity. It is also embedded in supervision, academic hierarchy, temporary contracts, archive infrastructure, peer review, funding/reporting systems, authorship and credit, publication prestige, disciplinary status and private identity. These forces can shape which questions seem safe, which counterevidence is expensive to acknowledge, whose invisible work becomes visible, how quickly a manuscript is declared mature and whether correction feels like learning or status loss.

The Role World therefore makes the social cost of good research visible without letting social approval become evidence. Academic prestige can change access, attention and expectations; it cannot make a source authentic, make a finding true, confer a degree, satisfy `qualification_required`, grant employment, approve ethics/privacy, award funding or guarantee publication.

## Situated reputation

There is **no global reputation score**. Standing is kept separately with:

1. supervisors and research leads — question scope, method and intellectual independence
2. archive and source stewards — provenance, access, context and source respect
3. peer reviewers and method peers — argument strength, falsifiability, counterevidence and review trust
4. project administration, ethics and funders — scope, reporting, ethics boundaries and resource truth
5. coauthors and research collaborators — credit, handoff, invisible work and revision fairness
6. editors, publishers and academic gatekeepers — claim strength, revision transparency and publication reliability
7. disciplinary peers and future employers — professional judgment, correction and portfolio trust
8. private relations — recovery, confidentiality, identity and status boundary

The same decision may increase standing with one audience while decreasing it with another. A delayed manuscript can frustrate a project interface while strengthening peer trust because a source group was actually checked. A major revision can cost short-term status while demonstrating that counterevidence changes the argument. An explicit ethics stop can strain delivery expectations without granting anyone else authority to decide the historical result.

No standing can create evidence, authenticate a source, decide a historical finding, confer academic rank, bypass `qualification_required`, grant hiring, funding, ethics/privacy approval or publication acceptance.

## Editorial uniqueness

The 14-day season is research-specific rather than a renamed leadership world. Its recurring pressures are:

- question scope and supervision without advisor-as-truth-machine
- archive provenance, access, representation and silence
- thesis attachment, sunk cost and counterevidence
- rival explanations, historiographic disagreement and peer review
- funding/reporting pressure versus epistemic maturity
- authorship, credit and invisible research labour
- citation control, ethics/privacy and correction
- publication prestige, rejection and bounded revision
- precarity, future employability and status anxiety
- private recovery without leaking confidential source, review or personnel material

The season contains 14 days × four phases (`task`, `relationship`, `decision`, `private_consequence`) = **56 unique authored beats**, seven long-running threads, eight delayed consequences and five private aftermath arcs. Every one of the 15 canonical prerequisite mails is used at least three times as delivery provenance.

## History Go boundary

History Go can improve questions about periodisation, context, historiography, archive silence, source selection, representation and rival explanations. It can prompt the researcher to ask what evidence is missing, what would weaken the thesis, which categories are anachronistic and which voices are absent from the archive.

History Go cannot authenticate sources, decide historical findings, grant a degree or qualification, satisfy `qualification_required`, give ethics or privacy approval, allocate funding, hire or promote the player, guarantee peer-review acceptance or provide publication authority. The concrete source base, method, institutional process and actual qualified decision-makers remain authoritative.

## Cross-role boundary

Readiness classifies this role as `not_required_for_rollout` for cross-role work. No shared work object is therefore invented. Realistic interfaces with archivists, administrators, editors, coauthors or other researchers remain editorial relationships inside this one role unless a separately governed shared-work contract is later proven. Cross-role linkage is not a prerequisite for this rollout.
