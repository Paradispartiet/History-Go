# People of Places — Akershus festning batch 3 research

## Scope

Batch 3 is a selective fortress architecture and engineering pass for `akershus_festning`.

The gate is stricter than general importance in Norwegian architectural or military history. Every accepted person must have a direct and substantial documented role in planning, constructing, engineering or commanding Akershus festning itself.

## Canonical audit

Repository searches were run for proposed canonical IDs and full-name variants before files were created.

Accepted IDs:

- `hans_van_paeschen`

## Accepted candidates

### Hans van Paeschen

**Decision:** accept.

Store norske leksikon states that van Paeschen worked at Akershus from 1566 to 1570 and produced the plan that initiated the conversion of the old castle into a modern fortress. His work introduced major bastion elements including Kongens and Dronningens batteri.

This is a direct, formative and site-specific Akershus relation.

Source:
- https://snl.no/Hans_van_Paeschen
- https://snl.no/Akershus_slott_og_festning

### Hans van Steenwinckel den eldre

**Decision:** accept.

Steenwinckel prepared plans for Akershus and major bastion works were executed according to his project from 1593 to 1604, partly under his direction. Norsk biografisk leksikon describes his project as decisive for the later form of the fortress works.

This is one of the strongest architectural relations available for the site.

Source:
- https://nbl.snl.no/Hans_van_Steenwinckel_-_den_eldre
- https://snl.no/Hans_van_Steenwinckel
- https://snl.no/Akershus_slott_og_festning

### Isaac van Geelkerck

**Decision:** accept.

Store norske leksikon describes van Geelkerck as a fortress engineer who led building work at Akershus. Biographical sources also document several later proposals for improving Akershus and Christiania's fortifications.

His relation is direct engineering work at the fortress, not a generic connection through the Norwegian fortification service.

Source:
- https://snl.no/Isaac_van_Geelkerck
- https://nbl.snl.no/Isaac_van_Geelkerck
- https://nkl.snl.no/Isaac_van_Geelkerck

### Michael Smith Arentz

**Decision:** accept.

Norsk kunstnerleksikon documents Arentz as an engineer active at Akershus and lists several works on the fortress: Arméens depot, Artillerigården and other buildings or projects associated with the site. His signed 1827 map of Akershus further strengthens the direct place relation.

He represents the nineteenth-century transformation of the fortress area from active fortification toward military administration, storage and institutional use.

Source:
- https://nkl.snl.no/Michael_Smith_Arentz

### Anthony Coucheron

**Decision:** accept.

Store norske leksikon describes Anthony Coucheron as an important engineer officer in Norwegian fortress construction and states that his final command was as commandant of Akershus festning. He died at Akershus in 1689.

The commandant role gives a direct institutional and physical connection to the fortress, while his engineering career makes him thematically coherent with this batch.

Source:
- https://snl.no/Anthony_Coucheron

## Considered but excluded

### Wyllem Coucheron

**Decision:** exclude from this batch.

Wyllem Coucheron was an important fortress engineer and was ordered to prepare a fortification plan for Christiania. The available source is less precise about a consequential personal role in works at Akershus itself and notes that the expansion of Akershus continued according to earlier plans.

He therefore falls below the current direct-place threshold even though his broader national fortification significance is substantial.

Source:
- https://nkl.snl.no/Wyllem_Coucheron

## Batch decision

Batch 3 contains five people with strong direct Akershus relations across three major phases:

1. the sixteenth-century conversion from medieval castle to bastion fortress;
2. seventeenth-century professional fortress engineering and command;
3. nineteenth-century military architecture and institutional construction.

No candidate was added merely to reach a fixed batch size.
