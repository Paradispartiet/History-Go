## Situated reputation contract

There is no global reputation score. Standing must be audience-specific and may diverge across at least these audiences:

- `study_leadership`
- `seminar_colleagues`
- `assessment_sensor`
- `study_administration`
- `students`
- `academic_integrity_and_appeal_process`
- `academic_peers`
- `private_relations`

Examples of legitimate divergence:

- A demanding but transparent grading rationale can improve sensor and study-leadership trust while a disappointed student remains dissatisfied.
- Refusing to use personal agreement as a quality criterion can strengthen student and peer trust even when it makes seminar discussion harder to control.
- Escalating suspected misconduct without pre-judging guilt can look slower to teaching staff while strengthening institutional-process trust.
- Stopping unnecessary health-data collection can frustrate an informal attempt to solve accommodation quickly while strengthening administrative and student trust.
- Revising a course after systematic exam evidence can cost short-term status for admitting an alignment problem while strengthening longer-term educational credibility.

Standing can never grant or expand formal authority. In particular it cannot:

- turn personal agreement into a grading criterion;
- introduce hidden criteria after submission;
- authorize the instructor to decide a formal misconduct case or sanction alone;
- override complaint, impartiality, accommodation or privacy procedures;
- justify collection of unnecessary sensitive student data;
- turn a History Go badge or Professor tier into academic qualification, appointment or assessment mandate;
- turn Arne Næss into a fictional current colleague or normative authority for today’s pedagogy.

## Arne Næss boundary

`arne_naess` remains a canonical History Go factual target only, anchored to `maerradalen`. The knowledge task is read-only historical/philosophical context. Næss must not become a current sender, colleague, teacher, examiner, employer or authority for present-day academic decisions.
