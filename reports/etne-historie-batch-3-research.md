# Etne historie batch 3 — research

## Scope and duplicate audit

The current `main` branch was searched before file creation for the selected names, IDs and relevant spelling variants. No existing canonical place records were found for:

- `skanevik_gjestgjevargarden`
- `driftevegen_stordalen_roldal`
- `postvegen_rullestadjuvet`
- `folgefonden_minnesmerke_skanevik`
- `reichwald_snublesteiner_skanevik`

Batch 3 deliberately adds historical place types not already dominant in Etne batches 1–2: a trading/guesthouse complex, a medieval east–west route, a nineteenth-century postal road, a maritime disaster memorial, and a Holocaust memorial.

## Skånevik Gjestgjevargard

Kringom documents the old Skånevik trading and guesthouse settlement. Only two buildings survive from the wider complex. From 1785 Morten Rosendahl operated as merchant and innkeeper and probably built the surviving main building. Ivar Aasen stayed there in 1844 during dialect studies. After Chr. Bjelland bought the complex in 1908, the main building was used for female factory workers and later became known as Tippehuset.

Coordinate: `59.73128737155455, 5.92525891571817`, official Kartverket/Geonorge address point for Furehaugvegen 4, 5593 Skånevik.

Sources: Kringom; Fjord Norway; Brønnøysundregistrene; Kartverket/Geonorge.

## Driftevegen Stordalen–Røldal

Kringom identifies the drove road through Stordalen and Øyno and over the mountains to Røldal as a central east–west connection since the Middle Ages. Røldal church was an important pilgrimage destination, while the Røldal market drew travellers well into the nineteenth century. UT.no also links substantial parts of the modern mountain route to the old drove and pilgrimage road.

Coordinate: `59.75232, 6.20454`, representative Etne-side anchor at Øyno. This is a linear historical place, not a geometric centre of the whole route.

Sources: Kringom; UT.no; mapped locality data for Øyno.

## Postvegen i Rullestadjuvet

Etne kommune, Fjord Norway and Visit Norway describe the old nineteenth-century post road between Skromme and the farms by Rullestadvatnet. The approximately two-kilometre historical route was fully restored in 2009 and is associated with post farmers, cattle drivers and other travellers.

Coordinate: `59.88144, 6.46432`, representative start anchor at Skromme.

Sources: Etne kommune; Fjord Norway; Visit Norway; mapped locality data for Skromme.

## Folgefonden-minnesmerket

The steamship `Folgefonden` grounded on Trøskenesfluno near Skånevik in 1908. Twenty-six people died. A memorial by Arne Mæland was erected in Skånevik in 1993 with the names of the victims. The canonical place is the memorial, not the offshore grounding point.

Coordinate: `59.73343, 5.93264`, quay/ferry-terminal anchor in Skånevik. The memorial is documented in the quay park by the ferry landing; the offshore accident coordinate is deliberately not used as the marker.

Sources: Lokalhistoriewiki; Skånevik Fjordhotel; ferry-terminal mapping.

## Reichwald-snublesteinene

Jødisk Museum Oslo's Snublestein service documents stones at Skånevikvegen 10 for Hans, Edith and Harry Reichwald. Hans, a refugee from Vienna, lived with his family in Skånevik and operated a bicycle workshop. He was arrested in October 1942 and deported with `Donau`. Edith and their two-year-old son Harry were later arrested and deported with `Gotenland`; they were killed on arrival at Auschwitz in March 1943. Hans died in Auschwitz in January 1945.

Coordinate: `59.733018516381904, 5.934691072324555`, official Kartverket/Geonorge address point for Skånevikvegen 10, 5593 Skånevik.

Sources: Jødisk Museum Oslo / Snublestein.no; Kartverket/Geonorge.

## Editorial safeguards

- Gjestgjevargarden must not imply that the full historical trading complex survives.
- The two road places are linear historical places using representative anchors.
- Folgefonden's memorial marker must not be confused with the offshore grounding site.
- Reichwald coverage must keep the three named individuals visible and avoid describing the persecution as solely a German action divorced from Norwegian arrests and deportation machinery.
