## Theatre form and architecture

The expression **baroque theatre** describes the preserved theatre and stage tradition, including:

- a sloping stage floor;
- auditorium and balcony;
- actors' rooms and foyers;
- perspective scenery and historic stage equipment; and
- associated ball room and salons.

It does not mean that the exterior is Baroque architecture. The wooden building has a restrained classical, Empire-influenced exterior designed by engineer-major Balthazar Nicolai Garben.

## Historical timeline

- An earlier theatre from 1820 was destroyed in the Halden town fire of 1826.
- Work on the present building began after renewed organisation and share subscriptions in the 1830s.
- Fredrikshalds Teater was completed and opened on **18 March 1838**.
- Social rooms were added in 1850.
- Cinema adaptations in 1937 altered parts of the original entrance and internal arrangement.
- Restoration enabled renewed use from 1982.
- Further exterior and interior restoration was undertaken in 2014–2017.

The scenery collection is unusually important, but not every item was made for the 1838 building. Some scenery is older, and the collection also includes deposited scenery from the former Arendal theatre.

## Current use and access

The building remains a living venue for theatre, concerts, lectures, guided tours and other events. Preservation requirements limit the intensity and form of use.

Interior access follows current:

- performance and tour schedules;
- ticketing and booking requirements;
- preservation restrictions;
- staff instructions; and
- accessibility conditions.

The historic building contains several levels and stairs and has no lift. Accessibility differs between the entrance, social rooms, auditorium, balcony and stage areas.
