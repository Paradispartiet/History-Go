## Why this point

OpenStreetMap node `6259987033` is a named `Prestegard` farm/site point at the historic rectory. Lokalhistoriewiki independently places Rakkestad prestegård at `59.4127854, 11.3688136`, only a few metres away, and identifies the property as gnr. 5/1 at Kirkeveien 116.

The applied point represents the rectory complex rather than one uncertain building entrance. Publicly inspectable sources support the main-building history and address but do not provide a stronger named building footprint that should replace the site point.

## Identity resolution

The record represents:

- the historic and protected Rakkestad rectory complex;
- the surviving main building erected in 1797;
- associated protected yard buildings;
- the rectory's role as a representative, travel and postal site;
- Christian Frederik's visit in 1814.

It does not represent:

- Rakkestad settlement;
- Rakkestad church;
- the entire agricultural holding;
- a generic address point;
- an automatically public museum or visitor site.

## Historical controls

Allstad and Lokalhistoriewiki support the following chronology:

- the present log main building was erected in `1797`;
- it originally had two storeys;
- the upper storey was removed and the entrance altered in `1838`;
- Christian Frederik visited in `1814`;
- the rectory also served as a post office for several decades;
- the building was restored in `1922–1923`;
- the complex underwent major rehabilitation in `2022–2023`.

The year `1797` remains the canonical year because it identifies the surviving main building. The record does not use 1814 as a construction year.

## 1814 interpretation

The 1814 layer is retained as a documented royal visit and as part of the wider military and travel landscape around Rakkestad. Nasjonalarkivet documents that around 6,000 Norwegian troops were stationed in Rakkestad during the August campaign.

The record does not claim:

- that a formal cabinet meeting equivalent to the Spydeberg meeting occurred here;
- that the Constitution was adopted here;
- that the Convention of Moss was negotiated or signed here;
- that every military event in Rakkestad occurred on the rectory property.

## Heritage and access

Kulturminne ID `86067` identifies Rakkestad prestegård. Allstad states that the main building, storehouse and exteriors of later farm buildings are protected. Riksantikvaren's 2025 decision confirms that the main building remains under active Cultural Heritage Act management.

The property is active and includes residential and agricultural functions. Therefore:

- the coordinate does not grant access to the residence, yard, garden or farm buildings;
- tasks must remain on lawful public roads or use explicit permission;
- current signs, ownership and event arrangements take precedence;
- the radius is not a building, heritage, property, garden, agricultural or access boundary.
