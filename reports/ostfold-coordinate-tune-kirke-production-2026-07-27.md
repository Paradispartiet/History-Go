## Why this point

OpenStreetMap way `93608238` is the named building geometry for the standing Tune church. Its representative point matches Wikidata Q6495605, Church of Norway building ID 1039, Kulturminne ID 85685 and the address Tuneveien 43.

The current building is the strongest physical anchor for a record whose significance extends across several earlier church and runestone layers.

## Layered site chronology

### Documented kirkested

- A church at Tune is mentioned in `1080`.
- The surrounding cemetery is registered as medieval.
- Tune was a county church in medieval Vingulmork.

### Medieval church

- The medieval church no longer survives.
- It was demolished in `1863`.

### Church of 1865

- A new church was taken into use in `1865`.
- It was destroyed by fire in `1908`.

### Standing church

- The present stone church was designed by Heinrich Jürgensen.
- Parts of the 1865 walls were reused.
- It was consecrated on `30 December 1910`.

The canonical year changes from an approximate `1100` to `1080`, because the official parish source provides a documented first church-site mention. This year does not date the standing building.

## Tune runestone

The Tune runestone is part of the site's find history, not a standing object at the church.

- It was documented in the churchyard wall in `1627`.
- It predates the Christian church site by several centuries.
- The original was moved to Christiania in the nineteenth century.
- It is now held by the Museum of Cultural History in Oslo.

The canonical coordinate therefore represents the find context and former churchyard-wall association, not the present location of the original stone.

## Borgleden and landscape

Tune church lies along Borgleden on the ra ridge. The pilgrimage and power-landscape layer remains contextual; it does not replace the precise church-building anchor.
