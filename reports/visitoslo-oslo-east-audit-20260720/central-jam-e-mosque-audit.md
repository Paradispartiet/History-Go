## Source basis

Official World Islamic Mission documentation identifies the mosque at Åkebergveien 28B as a purpose-built mosque. The congregation was established in 1984; before the current building was completed it used temporary premises elsewhere in Oslo. The foundation stone was laid in 1991 and the building was ready for occupancy in 1994/95.

The organisation describes a 1,260 square metre building over three floors plus gallery, with two minarets and room for roughly 700 people. Its decoration includes tiles from Iran and Spain, Quranic calligraphy, Iranian carpets and a Turkish chandelier.

Store norske leksikon identifies Central Jam-e-Mosque as a mosque belonging to World Islamic Mission, opened in 1995, designed by architect Eigil Wæhle and the first mosque in Norway built as a mosque from the ground up. Oslo byleksikon likewise describes the Åkebergveien building as the oldest Oslo building erected specifically for mosque use.

## Representation decision

**Approve one canonical candidate: `central_jam_e_mosque`.**

The place should represent the purpose-built mosque and the World Islamic Mission congregation's physical institutional home in Åkebergveien. It should not represent Islam in Oslo generically, all Pakistani-Norwegian religious life, or every WIM branch.

The place has a strong History Go case through:

- the emergence and institutionalisation of Muslim congregations in post-war Oslo
- the transition from adapted temporary premises to a purpose-built mosque
- religious architecture and the visibility of minority religions in the cityscape
- transnational material and decorative traditions in a Norwegian urban building
- the relationship between congregation, voluntary work, architecture and public religious space

## Scope guardrails

Do not:

- treat the mosque as a generic symbol for all Muslims in Norway
- conflate World Islamic Mission with other Muslim congregations or mosques
- reduce the place to current prayer times or service information
- create separate overlapping markers for the organisation and the mosque building at the same address
