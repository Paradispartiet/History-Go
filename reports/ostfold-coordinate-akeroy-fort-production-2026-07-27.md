## Chronology

Hvaler municipality presents 1664 as the fort's establishment year. Other sources describe an initial fortification around 1660 and later reinforcement in 1675, 1744 and 1788. The canonical `year: 1664` is therefore retained as the official local foundation year, while the text presents the fort as an installation developed in several phases.

Sources also vary between 1807 and 1808 for the demolition. The production preserves this uncertainty rather than selecting a false exact date.

The state sold the remains in 1850. Organised voluntary restoration began in 1962 and became a major part of the fort's modern history.

## Strategic role

The fort was positioned to defend the maritime approach toward Fredrikstad and control traffic and hostile naval activity in the outer Oslofjord. Its location on a small islet shows how shipping lanes, islands and narrow waters formed part of the defensive landscape.

## Access and nature-protection framing

The fort is reached by boat. Akerøya lies within Ytre Hvaler National Park, and parts of the main island are subject to seasonal access restrictions. The fort, however, is located on the separate Festningsholmen. Restrictions documented for Akerøya must not automatically be treated as an exact rule for the fort islet.

Before a visit, users must check:

- the current official national-park and seasonal-restriction map;
- local signs and temporary closures;
- weather, wind and sea state;
- vessel and landing suitability; and
- the condition of walls, ruins and paths.

No gameplay task may require landing during a prohibited period, entering a closed area or climbing unstable masonry.
