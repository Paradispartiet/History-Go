# Etne historie batch 7 — research

## Scope and duplicate audit

Current `main` was searched before file creation for the selected IDs, names and relevant variants. No existing canonical place records were found for:

- `etne_prestebustad`
- `grindheim_steinkross`
- `grindheim_runestein`
- `skanevik_kyrkjestad`
- `fjaera_kapell`

The batch adds one embetsgard, two concrete early-Christian/medieval monuments, one long-lived church site and one twentieth-century fjord chapel.

## 1. Etne prestebustad

Allstad documents the property at Stødlevegen 80. The main residence dates from 1871 and was later connected to an older borgstove said to date from the seventeenth century. The property also includes an eldhus and stabbur from before 1870 and a ved/vognskjul. Five buildings are protected.

Coordinate policy: resolve `Stødlevegen 80, 5590 Etne` through Kartverket/Geonorge during branch integration and use that official address point.

Primary source: Allstad, `Etne prestebustad`.

## 2. Grindheim steinkross

Kringom documents a stone cross now standing just inside the churchyard wall at Grindheim. It previously lay in the wall and is traditionally said to have stood originally on Krossbakken. The source notes that stone crosses in several places may have served as gathering places before churches were built, but this is not presented as certain for Grindheim.

Coordinate policy: use the current physical location at Grindheim church. Do not mark Krossbakken as though the original exact position were securely known.

Primary source: Kringom, `Grindheim kyrkje`.

## 3. Grindheim runestein

Kringom documents the runestone beside the north wall of Grindheim church. The inscription memorializes Tormod Svidande and can probably be dated to around the middle of the eleventh century. The stone also bears a carved cross. The meaning of the byname `Svidande` is uncertain and must not be presented as solved.

Coordinate policy: use the current physical location at Grindheim church.

Primary source: Kringom, `Grindheim kyrkje`.

## 4. Skånevik kyrkjestad

Norges Kirker documents Skånevik parish from 1340. The earliest known church was a stave church, replaced by a timber church in 1682–1683. That timber church stood in the churchyard south of the present church and remained until shortly after the new church was taken into use in 1900. The current church was consecrated on 31 August 1900.

Coordinate policy: use the present church/churchyard complex as the marker for the broader historical church site. The record must not imply that the present building is medieval.

Primary source: Norges Kirker, `Skånevik kyrkje`.

## 5. Fjæra kapell

Norges Kirker documents the chapel on the north side of the river a few hundred metres east of the head of Åkrafjorden. It was built in 1912–1913 as the first church on the site and designed by H.S. Eckhoff. The building was restored in 1968.

Coordinate policy: resolve `Fjæravegen 47, 5598 Fjæra` through Kartverket/Geonorge during branch integration and use the official address point.

Primary sources: Norges Kirker, `Fjæra kapell`; Etne kommune, `Kyrkja i Etne`.

## Editorial safeguards

- `etne_prestebustad` is a historical embetsgard, not another Stødle church record.
- `grindheim_steinkross` and `grindheim_runestein` are separate concrete monuments and must not duplicate the general Grindheim church-site narrative.
- the possible role of Krossbakken as a pre-church gathering place remains explicitly uncertain.
- the Grindheim runestone date remains approximate and the meaning of `Svidande` is not asserted.
- `skanevik_kyrkjestad` distinguishes the medieval/early-modern church sequence from the present 1900 building.
- `fjaera_kapell` is treated as twentieth-century local church history, not as a medieval site.
