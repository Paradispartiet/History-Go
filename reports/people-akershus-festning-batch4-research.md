# People of Places — Akershus festning batch 4 research

## Scope

Batch 4 is a selective late-medieval and early-sixteenth-century power-control pass for `akershus_festning`.

The gate requires a direct and substantial relation to Akershus itself through command, possession, administration or documented defence of the fortress. General importance in Norwegian union history is not sufficient.

## Initial canonical audit

The first proposed pool was:

- Håkon V Magnusson
- Christian IV
- Hartvig Krummedike
- Håkon VI Magnusson
- Margrete 1.

Fresh `main` audit found existing canonical records for four of the five:

- `haakon_v_magnusson`
- `christian_iv`
- `haakon_vi_magnusson`
- `margrete_1`

Those existing records use the active subplace `akerhus_slott` in their current place arrays. Because `akerhus_slott` and `akershus_festning` are separate active place IDs in the repository, this batch does not duplicate those people or silently rewrite the place semantics.

Hartvig Krummedike was not already canonical and remained in the batch.

## Final accepted candidates

### Hartvig Krummedike

**Decision:** accept.

Store norske leksikon documents that Hartvig Krummedike took over Akershus festning and its slottslen in 1445. After losing the post in 1458, he returned as høvedsmann in 1461 and apparently remained in the position until his death in 1476/1477.

This is a long, direct and institutionally important Akershus relation.

Source:
- https://snl.no/Hartvig_Krummedike

### Henrik Krummedike

**Decision:** accept.

Store norske leksikon documents that Henrik Krummedike became lensherre/høvedsmann at Akershus after bishop Karl Sigurdsson died in 1487. His appointment was part of the struggle between the union monarchy and the Norwegian aristocratic circle around the Alvsson family.

Source:
- https://snl.no/Henrik_Krummedike

### Odd Alvsson

**Decision:** accept.

Store norske leksikon documents Odd Alvsson as høvedsmann at Akershus in the 1490s. The fortress was a direct power base in his political conflict with Henrik Krummedike and the competing union-political factions.

Source:
- https://snl.no/Odd_Alvsson

### Olav Galle

**Decision:** accept.

Store norske leksikon documents that the Norwegian riksråd installed Olav Galle as høvedsmann at Akershus in 1525, against King Frederik I's wishes. The dispute over the fortress became a concrete constitutional struggle over whether the riksråd or the king controlled the central Norwegian fortresses.

Source:
- https://snl.no/Olav_Galle

### Mogens Gyldenstierne

**Decision:** accept.

Store norske leksikon documents that Mogens Gyldenstierne took possession of Akershus in 1527 on behalf of Frederik I, became lensherre and stattholder, and defended the fortress when Christian II attempted to regain Norway in 1531–1532.

This is both a direct institutional relation and a major documented siege/defence relation.

Source:
- https://snl.no/Mogens_Gyldenstierne

## Other candidates audited

### Karl Sigurdsson

**Decision:** no new file.

Karl Sigurdsson has a direct and strong Akershus relation as bishop, riksråd and høvedsmann, but a fresh repository search found him already represented in the existing people data. No duplicate is created.

Source:
- https://snl.no/Karl_Sigurdsson

### Peder Griis

**Decision:** defer.

Peder Griis is documented as a lensherre connected to Akershus, but the available significance case is weaker than the five selected candidates. He is held for a later pass rather than added merely to increase coverage.

## Batch decision

Batch 4 adds five new canonical records:

1. `hartvig_krummedike`
2. `henrik_krummedike`
3. `odd_alvsson`
4. `olav_galle`
5. `mogens_gyldenstierne`

Together they show how possession of Akershus functioned as a practical instrument of political sovereignty, union control and military power between 1445 and 1532.
