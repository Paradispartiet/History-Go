# People of Places — Akershus festning batch 2 research

## Scope

This batch continues the strict Akershus festning People of Places series.

Required gate:

1. The person must have a direct documented relation to Akershus festning itself.
2. The relation must be historically consequential or substantial in duration.
3. Loose Oslo associations, later memorial associations and brief transit stays are not enough.
4. Existing canonical people must not be duplicated.

## Accepted candidates

### Christian Jensen Lofthuus

**Decision:** accept.

Lofthuus led the largest Norwegian peasant uprising of the Danish period. He was arrested in 1787, taken in irons to Akershus and later sentenced to lifelong fortress labour. He remained imprisoned at Akershus until his death in 1797.

This is a direct, long-term and historically important relation to the fortress as a site of state punishment and political control.

Primary research sources:

- Store norske leksikon, `Christian Jensen Lofthuus`
- Lokalhistoriewiki, `Kristian Lofthus`
- Akershus festning cultural heritage route, Barakkene section

### Ole Høiland

**Decision:** accept.

Høiland was one of the best-known prisoners in the history of Akershus. He served multiple periods there, escaped from the fortress after prolonged planning, was later imprisoned there again and died in his cell in 1848.

The relation is not incidental: Akershus is central to both his historical biography and the history of the fortress prison.

Primary research source:

- Store norske leksikon, `Ole Høiland`

### Gjest Baardsen

**Decision:** accept.

Baardsen was sentenced to lifelong penal labour in 1827 and imprisoned at Akershus festning until his pardon in 1845. His long imprisonment and literary work from prison make Akershus a central place in his biography.

Primary research sources:

- Store norske leksikon, `Gjest Baardsen`
- Store norske leksikon, `Akershus landsfengsel`

### Lars Hætta

**Decision:** accept.

After his death sentence following the Kautokeino uprising was commuted to life imprisonment, Hætta was transferred to the penal institution at Akershus in 1856. During his long imprisonment there he became a central contributor to the work of translating the Bible into North Sámi. He was pardoned in 1867 and later completed the work.

The fortress relation is both long-term and historically distinctive, connecting Akershus prison history with Sámi, linguistic and translation history.

Primary research sources:

- Store norske leksikon, `Lars Hætta`
- Norsk biografisk leksikon, `Lars Hætta`
- Store norske leksikon, `bibeloversettelser`

### Peder Hanssøn Litle

**Decision:** accept.

Peder Hanssøn Litle became commander of Akershus in 1536 and held the office until 1551. Large rebuilding and alteration works were carried out during his tenure, while several eastern Norwegian districts were brought directly under Akershus.

His connection is institutional, physical and consequential to the development of the fortress as a centre of royal administration after the Reformation.

Primary research sources:

- Store norske leksikon, `Peder Hanssøn Litle`
- Store norske leksikon, `Akershus slott og festning`

## Place-model note

The repository contains both active place IDs:

- `akershus_festning`
- `akerhus_slott`

This series targets `akershus_festning` as the broader fortress complex. Prison-history candidates therefore use `akershus_festning` only. Peder Hanssøn Litle also receives `akerhus_slott` in `places` because his office and building activity relate directly to the inner castle as well as the wider fortress.
