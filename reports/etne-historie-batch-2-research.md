# Etne historie batch 2 — research

## Scope

Second dedicated Etne history batch. The current `main` branch was searched before creation for the five proposed IDs and names. No existing canonical place records were found for:

- `grindheim_kyrkje_etne`
- `bruteigsteinen_etne`
- `duesteinen_etne`
- `postvegen_etne_skanevik`
- `gamle_akrafjordvegen`

The batch deliberately extends batch 1 without duplicating its five anchors. It adds one long-lived church site, two separate but contextually linked rock-art localities, one early postal route and one twentieth-century road-engineering landscape.

## 1. Grindheim kyrkje

### Decision

Create `grindheim_kyrkje_etne`.

### Evidence

Norges Kirker documents Grindheim as a church site first mentioned in 1326. The present timber church most likely dates from 1723–1724, replacing an older church with surviving evidence of stave construction. The church was extended in 1854 and restored in 1954–1955.

Etne kommune lists Grindheim kyrkje as the 1728 church in the municipality. The canonical text uses the more cautious Norges Kirker dating, `1723–1724`, while `year` is set to 1724 as a practical representative year.

### Coordinate

`59.66984, 6.00409`, church/building anchor from mapped church data. This must not be confused with the separate Grindheim church in Agder.

### Sources

- Norges Kirker: Grindheim kyrkje
- Etne kommune: Kyrkja i Etne

## 2. Bruteigsteinen

### Decision

Create `bruteigsteinen_etne`.

### Evidence

Store norske leksikon identifies Bruteigsteinen and Duesteinen in Stordalen as major Bronze Age rock-art localities. NVE calls Bruteigsteinen on Flåte one of Vestland's most interesting rock-art sites. Kringom describes the field as unusually rich and varied and specifically notes the rare tree motif. The canonical record avoids assigning certain meanings to the symbols.

### Coordinate

`59.713137, 6.145756`, mapped rock-art locality coordinate. Because this is an archaeological field rather than a building, the normal coordinate gate and map review should be retained.

### Sources

- NVE: 041/1 Etnevassdraget
- Store norske leksikon: Etne
- Kringom: Helgaberget / Bruteigsteinen material
- Fjord Norway: Hellerisningar i Etne

## 3. Duesteinen

### Decision

Create `duesteinen_etne`.

### Evidence

Store norske leksikon and municipal tourism material identify Duesteinen as one of the major/accessible rock-art localities in Stordalen. Its editorial role is not to duplicate Bruteigsteinen's motif content, but to represent the unusually dense concentration of Bronze Age rock art around Stordalsvatnet as a connected cultural landscape.

### Coordinate

`59.731658, 6.169339`, converted from the published DMS locality coordinate `59°43'53.97"N, 6°10'9.62"E`.

### Sources

- Store norske leksikon: Etne
- Fjord Norway / Region Sunnhordland: Hellerisningar i Etne
- mapped locality listing for Duesteinen

## 4. Postvegen Etne–Skånevik

### Decision

Create `postvegen_etne_skanevik`.

### Evidence

Kringom identifies the route between Skånevik and Etne as part of the north–south `Stavangerske Posttour`. Postal routes developed after the postal service became more firmly organized from the mid-seventeenth century and only became carriage roads in the nineteenth century. The route has been restored as a walking route. Etne kommune also lists Postvegen Etne–Skånevik as a managed outdoor area.

### Coordinate

`59.699744, 5.937062`, a representative route anchor converted from the published UTM coordinate for Varleite / Postvegen Etne–Skånevik. This is a linear historic place, not a claim that the whole route is concentrated at one point.

### Sources

- Kringom: Åkrafjorden
- Etne kommune: Friluftsområde i Etne kommune

## 5. Gamle Åkrafjordvegen

### Decision

Create `gamle_akrafjordvegen`.

### Evidence

Kringom documents the road along Åkrafjorden as a major engineering project built in 1940–1942, with roughly 1,000 workers active in 1940, extensive rock cuts, stone walls, tunnels and bridges, and German pressure to accelerate the work for employment and military reasons. Current Åkrafjorden material states that construction began in 1937 and describes the preserved historic section between Tjelmeland and Teigland.

The canonical record therefore distinguishes between pre-war project origins and wartime acceleration; it does not describe the road as solely a German construction project.

### Coordinate

`59.80673, 6.21894`, representative route anchor at Teigland, one end of the preserved old-road section. Because the place is linear, the marker is an access/representation anchor rather than a geometric centre of the entire route.

### Sources

- Kringom: Åkrafjorden
- Åkrafjorden destination material: The Old Åkrafjord Road
- mapped Teigland locality coordinate

## Editorial safeguards

- Do not confuse Grindheim kyrkje in Etne with Grindheim kyrkje in Agder.
- Do not assign certain ritual meanings to Bronze Age rock-art symbols.
- Keep Bruteigsteinen and Duesteinen distinct: the former is a particularly varied motif field; the latter helps represent the broader Stordalen rock-art concentration.
- Treat both historic roads as linear places with representative map anchors.
- Do not state that Gamle Åkrafjordvegen was wholly initiated or built by the German occupation authorities; construction began before the war and was accelerated during the occupation.
