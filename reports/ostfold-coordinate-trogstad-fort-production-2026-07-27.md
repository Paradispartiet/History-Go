## Identity decision

Trøgstad fort is a large, distributed mountain-fort complex. Indre Østfold municipality describes an area of approximately 430 decares containing:

- about 1.3 km of tunnels;
- overbuilt artillery positions;
- about 500 metres of trenches;
- excavated accommodation and supply rooms;
- two original artillery batteries; and
- an outer infantry-defence system.

A single bunker or tourist-attraction building cannot represent this complete place. The canonical anchor therefore uses the named fort entity `Q19393412` at `59.63389, 11.34139`.

## Historical timeline

The fort was built in 1912–1917 as part of the Fossum-sector fortifications and opened in 1917.

On 13 April 1940, the fort fired against German forces advancing from the north along the road from Båstad and later toward Slitu. The fort surrendered on 15 April and remained occupied during the war.

Post-war uses included:

- ammunition storage;
- radar station and school from 1945 to 1956;
- army ammunition workshop from 1955 to 1995; and
- air-defence radar-control area for the Nike battery from 1959 to 1991.

The operational defensive role ended with the Nike shutdown around 1990–1991. This must be distinguished from the end of all military activity, which the municipality dates to 1995.

The municipality became owner in 2001. The site now supports heritage interpretation, school activities, recreation, tours and public events.

## Access and representation

Current signs, closures, event plans and guided-access requirements take precedence.
