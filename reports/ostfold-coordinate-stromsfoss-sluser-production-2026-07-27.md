## Identity decision

The place represents Strømsfoss lock, the single chamber and blasted canal built in 1857–1860. It does not represent:

- the broader Strømsfoss settlement;
- Strømsfoss road bridge;
- the natural waterfall;
- the mill and power environment;
- the lockmaster residence or the accommodation unit Fjøset;
- the manual precipitation station established in 1883.

These remain historically related components with separate geometry and access conditions.

## Physical configuration

Strømsfoss has one lock chamber controlled by two gates and a total lift of approximately two metres. The channel was blasted into rock roughly 50 metres south-east of the natural river and waterfall.

This separation between the navigable channel and natural watercourse is central to the place identity. The waterfall, mill and bridge explain local energy and movement, but they are not the lock structure.

## Historical interpretation

The lock was constructed from 1857 to 1860 together with Ørje locks. The year `1860` is retained as the documented opening/completion layer.

The canal created a transport node where water traffic, road movement, trade, lodging and working life met. The lockmaster environment and later service functions are part of that broader place history.

The manual precipitation station beside the locks was established in 1883 and has unusually long observation continuity. It is a separate scientific-history component and must not be described as part of the original 1860 lock structure.

## Current use and access

Strømsfoss remains part of the navigable Halden Canal. Guest berths, service facilities, accommodation and other visitor functions operate under their own rules and seasons.
