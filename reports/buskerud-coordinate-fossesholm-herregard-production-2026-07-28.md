## Identity decision

The canonical place represents the protected Fossesholm manor courtyard and museum complex at Fossgata 49. The operational museum point is used as a stable representative anchor for the multi-building visitor site.

The following remain separate components:

- the principal manor building;
- the north and south courtyard wings;
- the stone and timber barns;
- the post house and clock tower;
- the reconstructed historic garden;
- the much wider historical Fossesholm estate.

## Chronology decision

`year: 1541` remains, but its meaning is made explicit. It is the conventional foundation year for Foss as the centre of Fossesholm estate under Peder Hanssøn Litle. It is not the building year of the present manor house.

The surviving six-building complex is mainly eighteenth century. The manor was modernised in rococo style in the 1760s, including important preserved interiors and painted wallpapers. The wider estate broke up after 1822, six buildings were protected in 1923, and the courtyard entered museum use after Eiker Historical Society's purchase in 1973.
