## Identity decision

The canonical object is the classical stone church completed and consecrated in 1844. The medieval stone church that previously occupied the site burned after a lightning strike in 1841 and was demolished. Reused stone and surviving inventory preserve material continuity, but the standing building is not a medieval church.

Spydeberg rectory is a separate canonical place approximately 669 metres south-east. Christian Frederik's 1814 meeting belongs primarily to that rectory identity and is not used to define the church coordinate.

## Historical layers

- circa 1200: medieval stone church and Saint Margaret dedication; approximate dating
- 21 October 1841: lightning fire destroyed the predecessor
- 1844: standing classical stone church completed and consecrated
- 1922: separate grave chapel built by the churchyard

The medieval font and bells dated 1595 and 1625 are treated as surviving inventory layers. Reused masonry is documented as material continuity rather than evidence that the standing building is medieval.
