## Role-specific season

The 14-day season carries one continuous management problem through four daily phases:

1. the first report separates measured delivery from unknown load;
2. capacity behind the staffing sheet becomes visible;
3. peace below and speed above create asymmetric loyalty;
4. Thomas forces a boundary between support, expectations and formal process;
5. the first week exposes the player as translator rather than omnipotent decision-maker;
6. the simple action plan meets owners, resources and follow-up;
7. the team asks what actually changes;
8. numbers become politics without ceasing to be facts;
9. the Thomas conversation returns through documentation and trust;
10. the second week tests whether the player still acts as an invisible buffer;
11. incompatible demands must be made decision-ready;
12. the cost must be allocated explicitly rather than hidden downward;
13. Gunnar Jahn provides a History Go affordance for asking better questions about reports, institutions and uncertainty without granting modern authority;
14. the season closes with bounded responsibility, explicit handoff and private role containment.

## Situated audiences, never one score

Standing is authored separately for executive management, peer leadership, the work team, the employee in follow-up, governance/control, owners/decision forums and private relations. Trust may rise with one audience and fall with another. No audience can grant formal authority, waive privacy or personnel process, authorize misleading reporting, or replace the mandate expressed in the two work grammars.

## Editorial uniqueness gate

All 56 summaries and all 56 standing consequences must be independently authored and unique. Shared boilerplate may state the invariant that Standing never expands authority, but it cannot substitute for a role-specific event, audience reaction or delayed consequence. Every beat binds exactly one canonical source and every selected source grounds at least two beats.

## History Go affordance

The Jahn source is used as historical context for the difference between a measured result, an institutional steering signal and the uncertainty a report must preserve. It unlocks a better question—what is observed, inferred, decided and still unknown—but never modern reporting authority, HR access, board power or investment mandate.
