# Etne historie batch 4 — research

## Scope and duplicate audit

The current `main` branch was searched before file creation for the selected IDs, names and relevant variants. No existing canonical place records were found for:

- `sorheimsmoen_gravfelt`
- `grindheimsveien_nord_gravfelt`
- `steine_heio_bygdeborg`
- `tesdal_gravfelt`
- `etnesjoen_forromersk_landsby`

The batch expands Etne's prehistory with two large grave-landscape anchors, one separate local cairn field, a second documented hillfort, and a major excavated settlement site.

## Sørheimsmoen gravfelt

Kringom describes the Støle–Sørheim–Austrheim terrace as a major prehistoric grave landscape that historically held a very large number of burial monuments. Sources distinguish older Bronze Age monuments from many younger graves on Sørheimsmoen and describe large Iron Age mounds and stone settings. The municipality's archaeological register also lists Sørheimsmoen as a grave-monument locality.

Coordinate: `59.671449, 5.977786`, published archaeological-site coordinate for Sørheimsmoen. The record deliberately describes a surviving part of a historically larger grave landscape rather than claiming that every recorded monument remains visible.

Sources: Kringom; Etne kommune archaeological register; Kulturminnesøk-derived site data; Megalithic Portal map reference.

## Gravfeltet ved Grindheimsvegen

The Grindheim–Rygg area historically contained one of Etne's densest concentrations of burial mounds and cairns. The selected marker represents a surviving northern concentration along Grindheimsvegen rather than the whole historical landscape. Published site mapping identifies a cluster of large round mounds at this point, while the municipal archaeological register contains numerous separate Grindheim and Rygg grave-monument entries.

Coordinate: `59.665749, 6.003080`, published site coordinate for the northern Grindheimsvegen concentration.

Sources: Kringom; Etne kommune archaeological register; Askeladden/Kulturminnesøk-derived records; Megalithic Portal map reference.

## Steine-Heio bygdeborg

Store norske leksikon identifies Heia on Steine as one of the hillforts in Etne associated with the Migration Period. The municipal archaeological register includes the Steine/Heio archaeological locality. As with many Norwegian hillforts, the precise function cannot be stated with certainty; the canonical record therefore keeps refuge, watch and organised defence as possibilities rather than facts.

Coordinate: `59.65622, 5.98042`. This marker was cross-checked spatially against several nearby archaeological sites with published coordinates: Sørheimsmoen places Steine-Heio roughly 1.7 km south, Grindheimsvegen Nord roughly 1.6 km southwest, and Tesdal roughly 2.4 km west. The resulting triangulated point is internally consistent but must receive explicit map/coordinate review before merge.

Sources: Store norske leksikon: Etne; Etne kommune archaeological register; published nearby-site mapping.

## Tesdal gravfelt

The municipal archaeological register contains multiple grave-monument records at Tesdal. Published site mapping identifies a concrete concentration of burial cairns in two local groups around a farm road. The canonical record treats this as a smaller local cairn field and deliberately contrasts it with the much larger terrace grave landscapes at Sørheim and Grindheim.

Coordinate: `59.653057, 6.023207`, published archaeological-site coordinate for Tesdal.

Sources: Etne kommune archaeological register; Askeladden/Kulturminnesøk-derived records; Megalithic Portal map reference.

## Den førromerske landsbyen ved Etnesjøen

Universitetsmuseet i Bergen excavated a major prehistoric settlement area at Etnesjøen in 2013. Around 1,800 structures were recorded on a 16,000-square-metre excavation field and 54 buildings from different periods and phases were identified. Sixteen longhouses were directly dated to the Pre-Roman Iron Age; the excavators interpreted this part of the settlement as a village with several simultaneous farms through multiple phases. The overall site spans activity from the Late Bronze Age into the Early Medieval Period.

The published excavation report is referenced as `Etnesjøen/Askeladden ID130869`, on Gjerde gbnr. 9/1 and Skjensvoll gbnr. 6/1 and 3. The selected marker uses Skjensvoll as a representative anchor for the broad excavation area; it does not imply that the archaeological field is a visible reconstructed village today.

Coordinate: `59.6612, 5.94483`, representative Skjensvoll-side anchor within the documented Gjerde–Skjensvoll excavation landscape. Radius is intentionally broad because the excavation area was extensive.

Sources: Universitetsmuseet i Bergen, `Landsbyen ved Etnesjøen`; University Museum excavation report reference; Søren Diinhoff, `Etnesjøen – en førromersk landsby på Vestlandet` (Viking 85, 2021).

## Editorial safeguards

- Do not imply that all historically registered grave monuments at Sørheim or Grindheim survive today.
- Keep the selected Grindheimsvegen marker distinct from the whole Grindheim–Rygg grave landscape.
- Do not assign Steine-Heio a single proven military function.
- Treat Tesdal as a separate smaller cairn concentration, not a duplicate of the larger terrace grave fields.
- The Etnesjøen settlement record represents an excavated archaeological landscape; the 54 identified buildings belong to multiple periods and were not all standing simultaneously.
