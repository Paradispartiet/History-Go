# Atlas Obscura Oslo Wonderkammer batch 8

## Representation decision

These Atlas Obscura entries are not new canonical map places. They are concrete, named, physically discoverable details attached to existing History Go places, so they are modeled as `actual_site_treasure` Wonderkammer chambers.

## Parent mapping

- The Devil of Oslo / Oslo-djevelen → `oslo_domkirke`
- She Lies → `operahuset`
- Liberté / French Toilets of Spikersuppa → `spikersuppa`

## Source basis

### Oslo-djevelen

Oslo domkirke's own historical material identifies the relief as the church's oldest treasure, dates the picture stone to around 1100, places it in the tower base to the right of the main entrance, and says it is assumed to originate from Hallvardskirken.

### She Lies

KORO identifies Monica Bonvicini's 2010 sculpture as a floating work outside the Opera, made from acid-resistant steel and glass, moored so that it can rotate around its axis, and based on Caspar David Friedrich's `Das Eismeer`.

### Liberté

Oslo kommune describes Lars Ramberg's `Liberté` as three French red-white-blue street toilets created from a 2003 competition proposal connected to the centenary of the dissolution of the union. Atlas Obscura was used as the audit lead; the History Go representation is grounded in the municipal description.

## Data rule

All three chambers use `treasureScope: actual_site_treasure`. No new map coordinates or canonical place IDs are introduced.
