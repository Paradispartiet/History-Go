# Atlas Obscura Oslo Wonderkammer batch 11

## Final unresolved Atlas objects

The final three Atlas Obscura Oslo objects are represented as Wonderkammer `actual_site_treasure` entries rather than new canonical map places:

- Myth (Sphinx) – Kate Moss → `folketeateret`
- Glory Glory with Crutches → `akershus_festning`
- Donald T.-benken → `oslo_bussterminal`

## Source confidence

### Myth (Sphinx)

Current 2026 visitor evidence still places Marc Quinn's sculpture in Folketeaterpassasjen. Atlas Obscura provides the detailed Oslo placement, while Marc Quinn Studio provides the artist's interpretation of the Kate Moss sculpture series as a culturally constructed image rather than a simple portrait. The Oslo-placement source quality is weaker than the KORO-backed works in earlier batches, and this is stated in the chamber metadata.

### Glory Glory with Crutches

Atlas Obscura and recent 2025 visitor/location evidence place Laura Ford's sculpture by Festningsplassen/Akershus. Laura Ford's official site documents the broader `Glory Glory` body of work. An authoritative Oslo collection/provenance source was not located, so the chamber explicitly records that the provenance should still be strengthened if a better institutional source appears.

### Donald T.-benken

The physical bench is corroborated by Atlas Obscura, Morgenbladet, the stone-furniture manufacturer and fresh 2026 Aftenposten reporting on Galleri Oslo. The historical point is not that the bench was created as a presidential monument: Thomas Wollnick's New York-themed furniture used Donald Trump as a late-1980s symbol of Manhattan real-estate and finance culture.

## Representation rule

All three objects belong to existing parent places and do not justify duplicate map markers. Their value is as discoverable material details and as historical/contextual prompts inside the parent PlaceCards.
