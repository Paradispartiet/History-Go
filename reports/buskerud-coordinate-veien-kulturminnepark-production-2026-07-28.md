## Identity decision

The canonical place represents Veien Kulturminnepark as a combined archaeological heritage park and museum complex. The named museum point is used as a stable representative anchor for the whole visitor site.

The following remain separate components rather than alternative canonical identities:

- the protected Iron Age burial field;
- the reconstructed longhouse;
- the museum and reception building;
- individual burial mounds and excavated settlement traces.

The coordinate does not assert a legal or archaeological boundary around the burial field.

## Chronology decision

`year: 200` is retained as an approximate chronological anchor for the Roman-period phase represented by several rich graves and settlement traces. It is not:

- an exact date for the burial field;
- the construction year of the reconstructed longhouse;
- the opening year of the museum;
- the beginning or end of all activity at Veien.

The archaeological landscape spans the beginning of the Common Era through approximately 400 CE for its principal grave phase, with later finds also documented. Oluf Rygh's investigations in 1870–1871 form a separate research-history layer.

## Access and safety

- Museum interiors follow announced opening times, tickets and organised activities.
- The burial field normally has outdoor public access on prepared paths.
- Visitors must not walk on or disturb burial mounds and archaeological structures.
- Groups should contact the museum before organised use.
- Grilling requires explicit agreement, and ordinary leash rules apply.
- The gameplay radius is not a heritage, property, museum or access boundary.
