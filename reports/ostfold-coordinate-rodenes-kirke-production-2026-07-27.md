## Dating and building phases

Marker municipality dates the Romanesque stone church to **around 1230**. Marker parish uses the safer formulation **the first half of the thirteenth century**. Some registry metadata gives 1275, but the production does not treat that value as a certain construction year.

The retained `year: 1230` is therefore an approximate local-authority date.

The major later building phase occurred in 1703–1709, when the church was lengthened and received:

- the present timber-framed tower;
- a western porch; and
- a northern sacristy.

Medieval roof construction and parts of the eastern gables remain preserved. The church was restored in 1949–1952.

## Site and historical context

The church stands on a headland on the western side of Rødenessjøen, east of the rectory. The wider setting includes the churchyard, rectory landscape, shoreline and former boat-landing traces.

The altarpiece from the 1720s is connected to Christian Hansen Sarpsborg, a former commandant at Basmo fortress. This provides a concrete relationship between church patronage and the military and social elite in Marker.
