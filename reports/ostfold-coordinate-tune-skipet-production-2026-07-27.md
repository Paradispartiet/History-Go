## Coordinate decision

The canonical marker now follows the named archaeological-site point `Tuneskipet` at the marked Båthaugen findspot on Haugen/Nedre Haugen farm at Rolvsøy. The legacy point was approximately 1.26 kilometres southwest and had no Coordinate Source Contract.

The applied point is supported by:

- the named OpenStreetMap archaeological-site and visitor-attraction object;
- Store norske leksikon's identification of Båthaugen on Haugen farm as the 1867 excavation site;
- Fredrikstad municipality's identification of the ship as a Rolvsøy find;
- peer-reviewed archaeological research identifying Nedre Haugen and the Båthaugen burial context;
- local documentation of the present memorial at Haugen.

## Identity and representation

The record represents the **findspot**, not the preserved ship as a museum object. The ship is held at the Viking Age Museum in Oslo. The original Båthaugen had already been heavily reduced before the 1867 excavation and was not reconstructed, so the point is treated as a marked archaeological findspot and memorial anchor rather than surviving mound geometry.

The retained 260-metre gameplay radius covers the memorial and immediate find landscape. It is not an archaeological-locality polygon, legal protection boundary, original mound diameter or property boundary.

## Rejected candidates

- **Viking Age Museum / Tune ship in Oslo:** wrong object role and wrong municipality for this findspot record.
- **Haugen farm locality:** supports identity but is less precise than the named archaeological-site point.
- **Legacy coordinate:** approximately 1,264.1 metres southwest and not tied to an identifiable source object.
